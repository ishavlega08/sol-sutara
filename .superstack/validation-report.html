Crypto Necessity Check

"What breaks if you remove the blockchain?"
A lot. The core value is a shared, tamper-proof ledger that no single organization controls. With a centralized database, you need a trusted third party — which is exactly what competing orgs (a Tier-1 supplier vs. their Tier-2 vendors) refuse to agree on. Blockchain is the trust substrate, not a decoration. Solana specifically earns its place: 400ms finality and ~$0.0001/tx make on-chain writes practical at supply chain scale, unlike Ethereum where per-component writes would cost dollars. Verdict: blockchain is load-bearing.

  • TechnicalMEDIUM
    Graph traversal (recall simulation across millions of components) must live in the off-chain indexer, not the Solana program. The indexer exists but hasn't been stress-tested at realistic supply chain scale.
  • TeamMEDIUM
    No stated enterprise sales relationships or supply chain domain expertise. Cold outreach to Tier-1 OEMs is extremely slow. The fastest path in is through Tier-2 suppliers with existing OEM audit pressure (e.g. IATF 16949 compliance).
  • RegulatoryLOW
    Regulatory requirements vary sharply by vertical (CBAM vs UFLPA vs DSCSA). Trying to support all three simultaneously is scope creep. Pick one vertical for v1.

Integration vs. Custom Build

Custom program is the right call here. No Solana protocol exists that handles multi-org component graphs natively. OriginTrail is Polkadot-based — not portable. You must write the Anchor program.

However, maximize integration for everything that already exists:
• Helius RPC + DAS API — indexing, webhooks, compressed account reads (avoid running your own RPC)
• ZK Compression (Light Protocol) — replace Vec<u64> parents with compressed component links to scale to millions of components at <$0.001 each
• Solana Token Extensions — component identity / provenance certificates as non-transferable tokens
These integrations cut months of infrastructure work and reduce audit surface area.
