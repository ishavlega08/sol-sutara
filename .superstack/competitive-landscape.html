Dead Projects — What Failed and Why

TradeLens — Maersk + IBM (2018–2023)

A $500M+ blockchain consortium for global shipping built on Hyperledger Fabric. Shut down Q1 2023 after Maersk stated: "the need for full global industry collaboration has not been achieved." Competing shipping lines (MSC, CMA CGM) refused to share data on a platform controlled by their rival Maersk. IBM and Maersk absorbed the full loss.

→ Lesson for Sol Sutara: Perceived centralization kills multi-org supply chain products. A Solana-based neutral ledger — where no single org controls the program — directly solves the TradeLens failure mode. This is your strongest counter-argument to "why not just use a shared database?"

ASX CHESS Blockchain Replacement (2016–2022)

Australian Securities Exchange spent $168M and 6+ years on a blockchain-based clearing system before cancelling in November 2022. Primary failures: overscoped, underdelivered technical complexity, and poor stakeholder coordination.

→ Lesson: Start narrow. One vertical (India auto suppliers), one use case (recall tracing), prove it works, then expand.

Non-Crypto Substitutes — What Users Do Today

SAP Ariba / Oracle SCM

Why users stay: deep ERP integration, existing contracts

5.3M+ companies connected on Ariba. But these are procurement tools, not tamper-proof multi-org trace graphs. No cross-org component lineage. Recall tracing is manual and takes weeks. Switching cost is very high — this is Sol Sutara's biggest displacement challenge.

Spreadsheets + Email ("Manual EDI")

Why users stay: free, familiar, no IT project required

The actual reality for most Tier-2/Tier-3 India MSME suppliers. Excel files emailed back and forth. This is the wedge entry point — the pain is visceral and the ROI of switching is obvious. They have zero switching cost.

TraceX Technologies (India)

Why users stay: India-local, agri compliance focus

Bengaluru-based, $1.19M raised, focused on agri/food traceability in India. Not industrial. Interesting signal: even a seed-stage India supply chain startup has 18 investors — the India compliance market is real and local players get first looks.

TrusTrace / Sourcemap (Fashion)

Why users stay: fashion vertical compliance (CSRD/EUDR)

Fashion and textile traceability is a separate vertical with different regulatory drivers. Not a direct substitute, but shows the playbook: pick one regulated vertical, become the compliance default, expand from there.

Moat Analysis

Primary Moat: Network Effects (Multi-sided)

How it works
Every organization that joins Sol Sutara and adds components to the graph makes the graph more valuable to every other org. When Mahindra's Tier-2 suppliers are on-chain, Mahindra has direct incentive to mandate their Tier-3 suppliers join. This is a classic supply chain flywheel: the OEM pulls the suppliers in.
Time to become meaningful
3–5 pilot orgs in a single supply chain (e.g., 1 OEM + 4 Tier-2 suppliers). At that point, the graph has enough data to demonstrate recall simulation. Estimated: 6–12 months from first enterprise contract.
Secondary Moat: Switching Costs
Once a supplier's component history is on-chain (component IDs, batch records, link graph), migrating to another system means losing that history or running a painful export process. The longer orgs use Sol Sutara, the more locked in they become.
Tertiary Moat: Data Intelligence
The graph becomes a proprietary intelligence asset: which components fail most, which suppliers have cross-client quality issues, what the risk profile of a new supplier looks like. OriginTrail is building toward this (AI-ready data), but they're pivoting away from supply chain focus to be "AI infrastructure" broadly. Sol Sutara can own supply chain intelligence specifically.
Biggest threat to the moat
A well-resourced competitor (OriginTrail "Enterprise Paranets", or an SAP blockchain product) ports to Solana and targets India. You have roughly 12–18 months before this becomes a real threat, given enterprise sales cycle lengths.

Recommended Differentiation Angle

Own "Solana-native industrial traceability for India" — not "yet another supply chain blockchain"

  • Solana-native speed + cost: 400ms finality, ~$0.0001/tx. A single recall event touching 10,000 components costs $1 to trace on-chain. On Ethereum that's $1,000+. On OriginTrail's Polkadot DKG, latency and costs are higher. Lead with numbers.
  • Industrial-first, not food/agri: VeChain owns consumer goods. FarmTrack owns agriculture. The automotive + electronics + pharma verticals are wide open on Solana. Own them explicitly.
  • India market wedge: OriginTrail's live customers are Swiss, American, and European. Mahindra, Tata, Hero MotoCorp, and their supplier networks are unclaimed. India's PLI (Production-Linked Incentive) scheme is creating regulatory pressure for domestic traceability. First-mover advantage is real here.
  • Neutral ledger (TradeLens lesson): No single org controls the Solana program. Competing suppliers can share a ledger they all trust. Make this explicit in every enterprise conversation — it's why TradeLens died and why Sol Sutara can't die the same way.
  • Intelligence layer (recall simulation): No competitor has a live recall simulator. When you can demo "inject a defective batch → instantly see all 847 affected products across 6 organizations" — that's the conversation-stopper that closes enterprise pilots.
